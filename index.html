Method & validation

What this page does

NEA's 24-hour PSI is the reference for Singapore's health advisories, which are based on day-long exposure, and NEA also publishes hourly PM2.5 concentrations openly. A 24-hour average naturally responds gradually: when haze moves in, the average rises over the following day, and short-lived peaks are spread across the window. This page is a curiosity-driven exercise in how much of the hour-to-hour picture can be recovered from the 24-hour figures alone, and it places NEA's own hourly readings alongside for comparison.

The forward model

Each region's 24-hour PM2.5 value matches the mean of the previous 24 hourly readings, rounded to a whole number (checked against NEA's hourly series to within ±0.25 µg/m³). The PSI is a piecewise-linear function of that concentration, so the estimate is made on concentrations and then converted to the PSI scale using NEA's published breakpoints.

Why simple algebra is not enough

In principle x(t) = x(t−24) + 24·[m(t) − m(t−1)] recovers each hour from the previous day. Because published values are whole numbers, small rounding differences are multiplied by 24 at each step and accumulate over days, so on its own this approach drifts within about a week. A 24-hour mean also carries no information about patterns that repeat exactly every 24 hours.

The estimate

For each region the page solves, over the last eight days,

minimise  λ·Σ|xt − xt−1|  +  μ·Σ max(0, |mean24(x)t − mt| − 0.5)   subject to  x ≥ 0

— the smoothest hourly series consistent with every published value to within rounding (λ = 1, μ = 50), computed in your browser with a pre-conditioned Chambolle–Pock algorithm. For the newest hour this is averaged with a closure estimate, 24·m(T) − Σ x̂(T−k), which helps it respond to rising haze. The hourly figure shown is the highest of the estimated PM2.5 and PM10 sub-indices and NEA's published O₃, CO and SO₂ sub-indices.

For reference, NEA's 24-hour PSI differs from the hourly readings by 26 PSI (RMSE) — expected, since the two measure different things: one a day-long average, the other a single hour. Across 120 region-days, the day's highest 24-hour value was on average 27 points below the day's highest hourly reading, as is typical of an average; the day's highest hourly estimate was on average 3 points below it (mean absolute difference 8). The shaded band is ±1σ from an empirical error model, σ(µg/m³) ≈ 5.5 + 0.074·x̂ (newest hour) to 4.3 + 0.073·x̂ (≥3 h old).

Please note

The hourly figures are estimates for interest only. They are not an official index, and NEA's 24-hour PSI and health advisories remain the reference for decisions — see haze.gov.sg. The PM10 estimate has no hourly reading to compare against. When haze rises quickly, the newest-hour estimate tends to run about 10–20% low and is refined over the next few hours. Not for medical or operational use.

With thanks to NEA and data.gov.sg for making these readings openly available.
